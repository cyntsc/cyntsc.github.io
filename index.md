For the past eight years I have dedicated most of my career to exploring transcriptomics, and more recently epigenomics, spanning bulk RNA, single-cell, spatial, and multiome (scRNA-seq + scATAC-seq) analyses. I have run a wide range of pipelines and workflows aimed at addressing key biological questions across two distinct fields: plant–pathogen interactions and human brain neuroscience. Odd mix? Perhaps. But while they may seem worlds apart, I see many shared genomic fundamentals.

This notebook is my digital lab bench: a place to document workflows, record unexpected findings, troubleshoot challenges, and capture insights that emerge along the way. It serves both as a personal reference and an open resource for others navigating similar data complexities.

If you’d like to know more about my background and interests, visit [More about me](/more-about-me.md/).

## What you’ll find here

Curated references that have been most valuable in my work, short technical write-ups and interpretations of common and less common visualizations about scRNA-seq, Multiome (snRNA-seq + snATAC-seq), and Visium Spatial Transcriptomics.

## Why share this?

Every neat figure or polished manuscript hides a trail of trial, error, and “why on earth did I think that would work?” moments. By sharing my notes, I hope to:

- **Show the kind of experience (and occasional stubbornness)** it takes to get from raw data to conclusions.
- **Help others dodge some of the same pitfalls** (or at least feel less alone while falling into them).
- **Build a transparent portfolio** of how I think through data from first QC plots all the way to that hard-won last figure.
- **Gather links to papers, vignettes, blog posts, Twitter threads, and other resources** that have shaped my work (and probably saved me from disaster more than once).

> **Important:**  
> This isn’t a complete start-to-finish guide for replicating full pipelines. Honestly, there are hundreds of excellent resources out there already, many much better than I could write. Plus, each project comes with its own technical quirks and biological context. Most of my actual solutions are **pieced together from a tent of decent references** to fit specific problems.

If you spot mistakes, have suggestions, or just want to geek out about data, your feedback is very welcome. I’m always tweaking things here to make it more useful — for myself and hopefully for others.
